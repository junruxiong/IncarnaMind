# Retrieval uses node:sqlite, in-memory vectors, multilingual-e5-small and segmented FTS5

The retrieval prototype (issue #21, `prototype/retrieval/` on the `prototype/retrieval` branch) settled the storage and search choices that ADR-0008 and spec #20 left open. The numbers come from an evaluation set of 20 questions (10 English, 10 Chinese) over the seven sample PDFs and five Chinese Wikipedia articles, plus 5 cross-lingual questions reported separately. A question counts as a hit when one of the top 5 Passages covers the expected page and contains the expected quote. Timings are from an Apple M2 Max (12 cores). With 10 questions per language, one question is 10 points, so a difference of one question is not evidence.

- **Driver: `node:sqlite`.** It loads sqlite-vec and runs FTS5 in an Electron 44 main process and in a utility process on macOS, with no extra package. On Electron's Node 24.21 it matched better-sqlite3 on lookups (about 135,000 a second each), vector queries (6.1 against 6.6 ms) and keyword queries (19 against 18 ms), and both bundle SQLite 3.53.4. Bulk inserts were 1.4 times slower (2.0 s against 1.4 s for 10,000 Passages with their keyword and vector rows), which is small next to the time spent embedding them. Electron 44 prints no experimental warning for it.
- **Vectors: brute-force cosine in memory, not sqlite-vec.** Embeddings are stored as float32 blobs on the Passage rows and scanned in memory. Both approaches return identical top 50s. At the sample corpus (1,806 Passages) a query takes 0.7 ms in memory against 1.3 ms in sqlite-vec. At 100,000 Passages of 384 dimensions it takes 40 ms against 62 ms, and a query limited to a twelfth of the Passages (a Search scope) takes 4.6 ms against 34 ms. The cost is 154 MB of memory at 100,000 Passages and 0.2 to 0.3 s to load them from SQLite.
- **Built-in embedding model: multilingual-e5-small, int8 ONNX** (`Xenova/multilingual-e5-small`, MIT, 118M parameters, 384 dimensions, 135 MB download). At the old 400/200 Passages it found 14 of the 20 questions with vector search alone (5 English, 9 Chinese), against 15 for bge-m3, the best and largest candidate. bge-m3 is 4.3 times the download and took 6.5 times as long to index (418 s against 64 s for the sample corpus). e5-small indexes about 22 to 28 Passages a second on this CPU (18 with 4 threads) and peaks at about 0.9 GB of memory. The int8 model scored the same as the fp32 one at about a quarter of the download.
- **Passages: about 500 tokens with 200 overlap**, still prefixed with the Document's name, with the sliding window of 3 (step 1) unchanged. With e5-small, 500/200 found 15 of 20 with vector search and 17 of 20 with hybrid search, against 14 and 15 at the old 400/200, and it needs a third fewer Passages (1,204 against 1,806). Token counts stay approximate: one per CJK character, four characters per token otherwise.
- **Keyword search: FTS5 with the default `unicode61` tokenizer over text pre-segmented with `Intl.Segmenter`**, fused with vector search by reciprocal rank fusion (k = 60). With e5-small at 500/200, hybrid search found 17 of 20 against 15 for vector search alone: English rose from 5 to 8 of 10 and Chinese went from 10 to 9. `Intl.Segmenter` is part of the ICU data in Node and Electron, so this adds no dependency, and the index stays the size of a plain `unicode61` index (1.1 MB for the sample corpus).
- **Schema tooling: plain SQL migrations with a small runner.** The prototype's runner kept the version in `PRAGMA user_version`; the app's runner now also records each applied migration in a `schema_migrations` table, so migrations from tickets built in parallel can land in any order. The tables search depends on are FTS5 virtual tables, which Drizzle's migration tool cannot generate, and Drizzle's stable release (0.45.3) has no `node:sqlite` driver. Only the 1.0 release candidate has one.

## Considered options

- **better-sqlite3 13** is kept as the fallback. Version 13 is built on Node-API and ships prebuilt binaries for eight platforms, so it loaded in Electron 44 without `electron-rebuild`. It is faster at bulk inserts, but it is a native dependency we don't need.
- **sqlite-vec 0.1.9** works in both drivers and in Electron, but it was slower than a plain loop at every size measured, especially for scoped queries. It is pre-1.0, publishes no Windows on Arm binary, and would be one more native library to unpack from the asar archive and sign. Revisit it, or quantise the in-memory vectors, if libraries grow well past 100,000 Passages.
- **Other embedding models** (all int8 ONNX, at 400/200 Passages):
  - bge-m3 (MIT, 568M parameters, 587 MB) scored 15 of 20 and was the best across languages: 4 of 5 Chinese questions over English Documents, against 2 for e5-small. It is too slow to be the default on a CPU.
  - snowflake-arctic-embed-m-v2.0 (Apache-2.0, 328 MB) scored 14 and took 160 s to index.
  - gte-multilingual-base (Apache-2.0, 357 MB) scored 12.
  - paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2 (Apache-2.0, 135 MB) scored 10, because it reads only the first 128 tokens of a Passage.

  Users who need better cross-lingual search can switch to an API or Ollama embedding model (ADR-0005).
- **Passage sizes from 150 to 600 tokens.** Smaller Passages lost English questions: 200/50 found 13 of 20 with vector search and 12 with hybrid search. 600/200 matched 500/200 with vector search but scored 15 with hybrid search, and 68% of its English Passages are longer than the 512 tokens e5-small reads. 400/100 also reached 17 with hybrid search but only 14 with vector search.
- **The FTS5 `trigram` tokenizer** found 15 of 20 with hybrid search (English 6 of 10). Its index was six times larger (6.3 MB against 1.1 MB) and its queries were twice as slow (0.42 against 0.22 ms).
- **The default tokenizer without segmentation** scored 18 of 20 with hybrid search, but only because it treats a whole run of Chinese characters as one word, so it never matches Chinese text (0 of 10 Chinese questions with keyword search alone). For Chinese it is vector search under another name, and exact Chinese terms get no keyword help.
- **Drizzle 1.0 release candidate on `node:sqlite`.** Typed inserts and selects worked, but every search query needs raw SQL anyway and the release is not final. Revisit when Drizzle 1.0 ships if hand-written queries become a burden.

## Consequences

- The success bar in the v1 design (16 of 20, and 8 of 10 per language, with the built-in model) is met by hybrid search at 500/200 (17 of 20: 8 English, 9 Chinese) but not by vector search alone (15 of 20, with 5 English). The search Tool must run both.
- Chinese questions over English Documents are weak with the built-in model (2 of 5 with vector search, 1 of 5 with hybrid search). The model favours Documents in the question's language.
- The embedding utility process embeds Passages one at a time. Batches gave no speed-up, raised peak memory from 0.9 GB to 1.9 GB at a batch of 16, and made int8 embeddings depend on the other texts in the batch: cosine similarity with the unbatched embedding dropped as low as 0.991.
- The search Tool keeps the vectors for a Search scope in memory in the process that runs search, and must keep them in step as Documents are added and deleted.
- Integer parameters bound through `node:sqlite` must be `BigInt` where SQLite checks types (virtual tables, `STRICT` tables), because it binds every JavaScript number as a floating-point value.
- Text is normalised before it is embedded or indexed, not only when Citations are checked. Normalisation is NFKC plus folding of CJK radical look-alikes that NFKC misses, because PDFs made by browsers often encode `大` as the Kangxi radical `⼤` (8% of the Chinese characters in the fixtures). Whitespace next to CJK characters is removed, since pdf.js inserts spaces around Latin words and numbers inside Chinese text.
- Keyword search can't match simplified and traditional Chinese with each other. The embedding model covers that case.
- The window of 3 was not tuned. The hit rule scores single Passages, and the window only matters for the grouping step inside the search Tool.
- The Add Documents code (#25) marks its FTS5 trigram tokenizer and 400/200 Passages as provisional until this decision. Both should change to the values above in a follow-up, along with the text normalisation.
- The evaluation set and the Chinese fixtures live in `eval/retrieval/` (CC BY-SA 4.0 for the Wikipedia fixtures, see its `ATTRIBUTION.md`) for the retrieval evaluation ticket (#31). The prototype's harness stays on the `prototype/retrieval` branch.
