# Retrieval uses node:sqlite, in-memory vectors, multilingual-e5-small and segmented FTS5

The retrieval prototype (issue #21, `prototype/retrieval/` on the `prototype/retrieval` branch) settled the storage and search choices that ADR-0008 and spec #20 left open. The numbers come from an evaluation set of 20 questions (10 English, 10 Chinese) over the seven sample PDFs and five Chinese Wikipedia articles, plus 5 cross-lingual questions reported separately. A question counts as a hit when one of the top 5 Passages covers the expected page and contains the expected quote. Timings are from an Apple M2 Max (12 cores). With 10 questions per language, one question is 10 points, so a difference of one question is not evidence.

- **Driver: `node:sqlite`.** It loads sqlite-vec and runs FTS5 in an Electron 44 main process and in a utility process on macOS, with no extra package. On Electron's Node 24.21 it matched better-sqlite3 on lookups (about 135,000 a second each), vector queries (6.1 against 6.6 ms) and keyword queries (19 against 18 ms), and both bundle SQLite 3.53.4. Bulk inserts were 1.4 times slower (2.0 s against 1.4 s for 10,000 Passages with their keyword and vector rows), which is small next to the time spent embedding them. Electron 44 prints no experimental warning for it.
- **Vectors: brute-force cosine in memory, not sqlite-vec.** Embeddings are stored as float32 blobs on the Passage rows and scanned in memory. Both approaches return identical top 50s. At the sample corpus (1,806 Passages) a query takes 0.7 ms in memory against 1.3 ms in sqlite-vec. At 100,000 Passages of 384 dimensions it takes 40 ms against 62 ms, and a query limited to a twelfth of the Passages (a Search scope) takes 4.6 ms against 34 ms. The cost is 154 MB of memory at 100,000 Passages and 0.2 to 0.3 s to load them from SQLite.
- **Built-in embedding model: multilingual-e5-small, int8 ONNX** (`Xenova/multilingual-e5-small`, MIT, 118M parameters, 384 dimensions, 135 MB download). At the old 400/200 Passages it found 14 of the 20 questions with vector search alone (5 English, 9 Chinese), against 15 for bge-m3, the best and largest candidate. bge-m3 is 4.3 times the download and took 6.5 times as long to index (418 s against 64 s for the sample corpus). e5-small indexes about 22 to 28 Passages a second on this CPU (18 with 4 threads) and peaks at about 0.9 GB of memory. The int8 model scored the same as the fp32 one at about a quarter of the download.
- **Passages: about 500 tokens with 200 overlap**, still prefixed with the Document's name, with the sliding window of 3 (step 1) unchanged. With e5-small, 500/200 found 15 of 20 with vector search and 17 of 20 with hybrid search, against 14 and 15 at the old 400/200, and it needs a third fewer Passages (1,204 against 1,806). Token counts stay approximate: one per CJK character, four characters per token otherwise.
- **Keyword search: FTS5 with the default `unicode61` tokenizer over text pre-segmented with `Intl.Segmenter`**, fused with vector search by reciprocal rank fusion (k = 60). With e5-small at 500/200, hybrid search found 17 of 20 against 15 for vector search alone: English rose from 5 to 8 of 10 and Chinese went from 10 to 9. `Intl.Segmenter` is part of the ICU data in Node and Electron, so this adds no dependency, and the index stays the size of a plain `unicode61` index (1.1 MB for the sample corpus).
- **Schema tooling: plain SQL migrations with a small runner.** The prototype's runner kept the version in `PRAGMA user_version`; the app's runner now also records each applied migration in a `schema_migrations` table, so migrations from tickets built in parallel can land in any order. The tables search depends on are FTS5 virtual tables, which Drizzle's migration tool cannot generate, and Drizzle's stable release (0.45.3) has no `node:sqlite` driver. Only the 1.0 release candidate has one.

## Considered options

- **better-sqlite3 13** is kept as the fallback. Version 13 is built on Node-API and ships prebuilt binaries for eight platforms, so it loaded in Electron 44 without `electron-rebuild`. It is faster at bulk inserts, but it is a native dependency we don't need.
- **sqlite-vec 0.1.9** works in both drivers and in Electron, but it was slower than a plain loop at every size measured, especially for scoped queries. It is pre-1.0, publishes no Windows on Arm binary, and would be one more native library to unpack from the asar archive and sign. Revisit it, or quantise the in-memory vectors, if libraries grow well past 100,000 Passages.
- **Other embedding models** (all int8 ONNX, at 400/200 Passages):
  - bge-m3 (MIT, 568M parameters, 587 MB) scored 15 of 20 and was the best across languages: 4 of 5 Chinese questions over English Documents, against 2 for e5-small. It is too slow to be the default on a CPU.
  - snowflake-arctic-embed-m-v2.0 (Apache-2.0, 328 MB) scored 14 and took 160 s to index.
  - gte-multilingual-base (Apache-2.0, 357 MB) scored 12.
  - paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2 (Apache-2.0, 135 MB) scored 10, because it reads only the first 128 tokens of a Passage.

  Users who need better cross-lingual search can switch to an API or Ollama embedding model (ADR-0005).
- **Passage sizes from 150 to 600 tokens.** Smaller Passages lost English questions: 200/50 found 13 of 20 with vector search and 12 with hybrid search. 600/200 matched 500/200 with vector search but scored 15 with hybrid search, and 68% of its English Passages are longer than the 512 tokens e5-small reads. 400/100 also reached 17 with hybrid search but only 14 with vector search.
- **The FTS5 `trigram` tokenizer** found 15 of 20 with hybrid search (English 6 of 10). Its index was six times larger (6.3 MB against 1.1 MB) and its queries were twice as slow (0.42 against 0.22 ms).
- **The default tokenizer without segmentation** scored 18 of 20 with hybrid search, but only because it treats a whole run of Chinese characters as one word, so it never matches Chinese text (0 of 10 Chinese questions with keyword search alone). For Chinese it is vector search under another name, and exact Chinese terms get no keyword help.
- **Drizzle 1.0 release candidate on `node:sqlite`.** Typed inserts and selects worked, but every search query needs raw SQL anyway and the release is not final. Revisit when Drizzle 1.0 ships if hand-written queries become a burden.

## Consequences

- The success bar in the v1 design (16 of 20, and 8 of 10 per language, with the built-in model) is met in the prototype by hybrid search at 500/200 (17 of 20: 8 English, 9 Chinese) but not by vector search alone (15 of 20, with 5 English). The search Tool must run both.
- **Reranking by default (#31, 2026-10-09).** On the larger evaluation set (20 English and 20 Chinese Questions), hybrid search found 13 English Questions, short of 16. The search Tool now reranks by default with a built-in cross-encoder, mmarco-mMiniLMv2-L12-H384 (Apache-2.0, int8 ONNX, 136 MB, in a utility process of its own; ADR-0005). It doesn't rerank the fused list: it reranks keyword search's top 10 and vector search's top 10, each Passage once (16 on average), because fusion pushed out hits only one of them found. That found 16 English and 20 Chinese Questions, for about 0.5 to 0.9 s a search; bge-reranker-v2-m3 did the same at four times the download and twelve times the time, and gte-multilingual-reranker-base changed nothing. The retrieval gate is now this pipeline, what Users get; until the reranking model is downloaded, or when the User turns reranking off, search keeps the fused order.
- **Embeddings off by default (2026-10-10, decided with the User).** Search is keyword search (FTS5, BM25), its top 60 reranked by the built-in reranking model (its top 20 at first, see below), and a cross-lingual Question still gets its second search in the Documents' language. No embedding model is downloaded, loaded or run unless the User turns embeddings on in Settings → Document search, where "Off", the default, sits beside the built-in model and the providers; processing skips embedding, so a Document is ready, and searchable, once its keyword index is.
  - **Why:** the run of 2026-10-09 found keyword, vector and hybrid search level, 32, 31 and 32 of the 40 gating Questions, and level per format on the every-format set: the reranker, not the vectors, made the difference (36 of 40). Embeddings cost a 135 MB download, a process of up to 0.9 GB, and most of the time Documents take to process (130 s for the sample corpus's 1,199 Passages), and a cloud provider receives the text of every Document.
  - **Turned on,** Documents are embedded in the background as before, and search is hybrid search reranked: keyword search's top 10 and vector search's top 10. While the Search scope has no vectors yet, the reranker gets keyword search's top 60, as with embeddings off. While some Documents have vectors and others don't yet, hybrid search counts a Passage with no vector by its keyword rank for both lists, so having none neither drops it nor ranks it below Passages both searches found, as plain fusion would.
  - **Upgrades:** an install that had the built-in model, the old default, goes off; one where the User had chosen a provider (OpenAI, Google, an OpenAI-compatible server, Ollama) stays on. Vectors already made stay stored, unused, and are used again if the User turns the same model back on: only Passages without one are embedded. Documents that were waiting for the model are ready.
  - **The gate** (`eval/README.md`) is keyword + rerank, what Users get, with the same bar (32 of 40, and 16 of 20 per language); hybrid + rerank is reported beside it.
  - **Keyword search's top 60 for the reranker (2026-10-10, decided with the User).** With embeddings off, the reranker first got keyword search's top 20, as many as the most it gets with them on. That found 34 of 40 (15 English, 19 Chinese) and 10 of the 15 paraphrase Questions, against 36 and 11 for hybrid + rerank: it missed Passages keyword search ranks below 20 (en-12 at 71, zh-03 at 52, para-en-05 at 58 in its top 200). The evaluation then compared seven other ways to find the candidates, reported and never gating. Keyword search's top 60 found 36 of 40 (16 English, 20 Chinese) and 11 of 15, the same as hybrid + rerank, with no embedding model; the User's rule was to take the cheapest that matched hybrid + rerank, so the search Tool hands the reranker keyword search's top 60 (`keywordRerankCandidates` in `SEARCH_TOOL_PARAMETERS`), and the gate is that pipeline.
    - **Cost:** reranking 60 candidates takes the built-in model about 1.6 s a search on an Apple M2 Max, against about 0.8 s for 20. With embeddings on, nothing changes: keyword search's top 10 and vector search's top 10.
    - **Rewrites not by default:** a chat model writing 2 other phrasings of each Question in the Documents' words, each searched and fused with the Question's own search, found the most, 38 of 40, 12 of 15 paraphrase Questions and 8 of 10 cross-lingual ones, but it costs a model call before every search, 2.7 s on average with Claude Sonnet 5.5, and sends the Question to the model before any Passage is found. In an Answer, the model already writes its own search queries through the Tool-calling loop (ADR-0007), and may search again in other words or in the Documents' language. Feedback terms (RM3-style, no model), sub-questions, small-to-big sub-chunks and searching inside the best 3 Documents first found 34 of 40 each, no more than keyword search's top 20.
    - **The translated second search** for a cross-lingual Question stays: the search Tool still names the Documents' languages and tells the model to search again in them.
  - **Re-checked** by the evaluation's paraphrase row, 15 Questions worded unlike their passages, where embeddings should help most (the gating Questions mostly reuse their Documents' words, which favours keyword search), and by a harder tier of the evaluation being built. **Reopened** if hybrid + rerank finds more than keyword + rerank by more than one Question in a language on either (one Question is not evidence at these sizes, see above), or if Users miss Passages worded differently from their Questions.
  - The Topic grouping check (`npm run eval:grouping`), which needs vectors, turns embeddings on for its own library, as the evaluation does.
- The Passage sizes hold only with the prototype's way of building Passages. Whole lines fill a Passage up to the size, the next Passage starts with a tail of whole lines, and each line counts in whole tokens. The core first kept #25's builder at these sizes, which prefers to end at sentence and paragraph breaks. Its Passages were shorter, and hybrid search found 15 of 20 (6 English). Built the prototype's way, the app finds 16 of 20 (7 English, 9 Chinese; #31, `eval/README.md`). The one English Question still missed, en-05, was the prototype's fifth-ranked hit. Deliberate differences from the prototype tip it out:
  - onnxruntime-node 1.23.2;
  - the end-of-text token kept when a text is cut at 512 tokens;
  - the page text, without #30's headers and footers or NUL characters, and with CJK line breaks joined.
- Chinese questions over English Documents are weak with the built-in model (2 of 5 with vector search, 1 of 5 with hybrid search). The model favours Documents in the question's language.
- The embedding utility process embeds Passages one at a time. Batches gave no speed-up, raised peak memory from 0.9 GB to 1.9 GB at a batch of 16, and made int8 embeddings depend on the other texts in the batch: cosine similarity with the unbatched embedding dropped as low as 0.991.
- The search Tool keeps the vectors for a Search scope in memory in the process that runs search, and must keep them in step as Documents are added and deleted.
- Integer parameters bound through `node:sqlite` must be `BigInt` where SQLite checks types (virtual tables, `STRICT` tables), because it binds every JavaScript number as a floating-point value.
- Text is normalised before it is embedded or indexed, not only when Citations are checked. Normalisation is NFKC plus folding of CJK radical look-alikes that NFKC misses, because PDFs made by browsers often encode `大` as the Kangxi radical `⼤` (8% of the Chinese characters in the fixtures). Whitespace next to CJK characters is removed, since pdf.js inserts spaces around Latin words and numbers inside Chinese text. The Passages a chat model reads are stored text, not normalised text, but have the radical look-alikes folded (#67, 2026-10-10): every other reader folds them, and the model would otherwise read "⼤" where the page shows "大", in 8 to 11% of the Chinese fixtures' characters.
- Keyword search can't match simplified and traditional Chinese with each other. The embedding model covers that case.
- The Citation check and the viewer's highlight match quotes more forgivingly than indexing reads text. The evaluation run of 2026-10-07 with Ollama's mistral found 3 of 12 English Citations marked "not found" though their quotes were on the cited pages: a quote starting mid-sentence was given a capital, a paper's reference "[36]" was written "[^36]" in our Citation-marker syntax, and an ellipsis stood for a full stop and a heading. `findQuote` (`src/shared/quoteMatch.ts`) normalises the quote and the page text the same way: the shared normaliser, then, for matching only, letters in lower case, "[^N]" read as "[N]", a soft hyphen at a line end read as a line-end hyphen, and a few more quote marks and dashes unified. A line-end hyphen may also be followed by a space in the quote. `normaliseText` is unchanged, so indexing and embeddings are too, and no Document needs processing again.
- **An ellipsis in a quote marks words left out.** A quote with "..." or "…" that isn't on the pages as it is counts as found when each part is on the cited pages, in order, and every part has at least 3 words and 15 letters or digits; otherwise it is "not found". Shorter parts would match almost anywhere, and "found" must keep meaning that these words are on the page. The viewer highlights each part. The answering prompt asks for one unbroken stretch of a Passage instead. Nothing else is forgiven: a reworded quote, or one from another page, stays "not found". Re-checked this way, the run's recorded Citations have no false "not found" (`tests/eval/recordedCitations.test.ts`).
- The window of 3 was not tuned. The hit rule scores single Passages, and the window only matters for the grouping step inside the search Tool.
- The Add Documents code (#25) marks its FTS5 trigram tokenizer and 400/200 Passages as provisional until this decision. Both should change to the values above in a follow-up, along with the text normalisation.
- The evaluation set and the Chinese fixtures live in `eval/retrieval/` (CC BY-SA 4.0 for the Wikipedia fixtures, see its `ATTRIBUTION.md`) for the retrieval evaluation ticket (#31). The prototype's harness stays on the `prototype/retrieval` branch.

## Amendment: the Citation check folds what PDFs and Answers write differently (accepted, 2026-10-10, #67)

Status: accepted by the User on 2026-10-10: simplified and traditional folding, and f-ligature leniency only where a Document's stored text shows lost ligatures. It changes what "Quote found" means.

- **Simplified and traditional Chinese, character by character.** The Chinese fixtures are written partly in traditional characters (10 of the 20 Chinese gating Questions' answers), the Questions in simplified ones, and an Answer quotes in the characters it writes: qwen3.5:4b's quotes of traditional pages were "not found" though word for word but for the script (ADR-0007). The check (`findQuote`, `src/shared/quoteMatch.ts`, so the viewer's highlight too) now reads each traditional character as its simplified one, on both sides: the page's text and the quote. The table is OpenCC's `TSCharacters.txt` (Apache-2.0), from the `opencc-data` package 1.4.2 (Apache-2.0, 1.4 MB, a devDependency only, maintained by OpenCC's authors), turned by `scripts/hanVariants.ts` into `src/shared/hanVariants.ts`: 2,966 pairs, 19 KB, each one UTF-16 code unit to one, so offsets hold. Characters only: a phrase another region writes with other characters ("披露" for "揭露") stays "not found", as any other word does. Keyword search and embeddings are unchanged. Later on 2026-10-10, keyword search came to fold the two scripts too, because with embeddings off by default nothing else bridges them and the hard tier found hybrid + rerank 2 Chinese Questions ahead of keyword search's top 60 + rerank: its index and its queries read each traditional character as its simplified one with the check's own table, before the text is split into words, while the stored text keeps its characters (`src/core/documents/keywords.ts`; processing version 6 processes again only the Documents with Han characters in their text or name).
- **f-ligatures a PDF's text lost, only where the Document's text shows it lost them.** pdf.js reads some PDFs' "fi", "ff" and "ffi" ligatures as their first letter: JP Morgan's ESG report has "fnance", "eforts" and "ofce" in its stored text (751 words) where its pages show "finance", "efforts" and "office", so a quote of the page as it shows was "not found". A plain rule, a quote's "fi", "fl", "ff", "ffi" or "ffl" matching a lone "f" anywhere, was measured on the gating set's 11 other Documents: it would let 3.8% of their words (5,286 of 140,653) match another word, mostly "of" an "off", and also "few" a "flew", "food" a "flood", "four" a "flour", "feet" a "fleet". That is a word changed, so the rule is gated: a Document's text lost its ligatures when it has at least 200 "f"s before a letter and fewer than 5% of them come before "f", "i" or "l" (`lostLigatures`, `src/shared/quoteMatch.ts`). The Documents that keep them, with enough "f"s to tell, have 24 to 39%, JP Morgan's 1.3%; a page alone is too noisy to tell (pages of a reference list or a table have none in Documents that keep them). In such a Document only, a quote not found as it is is looked for with each ligature's letters read as one "f". The core works it out once per Document version from all its stored text, for the check, the Location's label and placing a record; the PDF viewer works it out from the PDF's text, only when a quote isn't found as it is, so its highlight agrees with the check.
